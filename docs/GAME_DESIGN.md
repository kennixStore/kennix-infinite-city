# Game Design — KENNIX: INFINITE CITY

This document separates the user's chosen direction from ideas that are still proposals. A proposal must not be treated as final until discussed and approved.

## 1. Identity and genre

- **Title:** KENNIX: INFINITE CITY
- **Genre:** Open-world action, driving, racing and adventure.
- **Delivery goal:** Playable through a website/browser.
- **Visual target:** Cinematic 3D, realistic-feeling roads and detailed vehicles.
- **Originality:** Broad genre inspiration is acceptable; copied maps, characters, logos, artwork, missions, music, code or distinctive sequences are not.

The slogan "One City. Endless Possibilities." is a working suggestion, not yet approved.

## 2. Agreed gameplay direction

- A mix of open-world exploration, endless racing and mission-based racing.
- Vehicle range should grow over time: everyday cars, SUVs, supercars, motorcycles, trucks and other classes.
- Vehicle customisation may include paint, rims, spoilers, neon, engine, nitro and performance tyres.
- Advanced damage is a target: visible damage, broken lights, crash consequences and garage repairs.
- Vehicle theft and robbery are intended game mechanics, including online-player interactions subject to fair protections.
- Player-created/customised characters are desired.
- Camera options: third-person driving view and first-person/cockpit view.
- Control goals: touch controls, phone tilt, keyboard and external controllers.
- Sound goals: engines, tyres, horns, crashes, nitro, environment, music and optional radio.
- KX is the fictional game currency. Players should be able to earn, spend and transfer it.
- Monetisation may include optional rewarded ads, restrained advertising and optional purchases; ordinary gameplay should not be interrupted by forced ads.

## 3. World and progression direction

- The world may combine fictional cities with real-world-inspired places.
- Cities are intended to unlock through progression; the first city should be freely explorable.
- Dynamic weather, day/night and environmental effects are long-term goals.
- Progression goals include levels, XP, KX, achievements, events, challenges, rankings and optional VIP features.
- The long-term multiplayer vision includes friends, private competitions and global competitions.

## 4. Fair-play requirements to design before online launch

Because players may steal, rob or transfer KX between real accounts, online systems need safeguards before release. Design requirements include:
- Clear ownership and recovery rules for personal vehicles.
- Anti-harassment protections and limits on repeated targeting.
- Transaction records and abuse detection for KX transfers.
- Server-side validation of balances, rewards and competitive results.
- Reporting, blocking and moderation tools for online interactions.
- No real-money cash-out unless a separate legal, financial and platform review is completed.

## 5. Opening cinematic

The target intro length is 60 seconds, using a mix of a continuous camera journey, dramatic action and character moments. An unexpected citywide blackout concept was proposed, but the exact sequence is not locked. A skip option after first viewing is also only a proposal.

The cinematic must be original and must not reproduce another game's exact camera route, shots, logo reveal or soundtrack.

## 6. Unconfirmed proposals — discuss before locking

- First city working name: **NOVA CITY**.
- District ideas: Downtown, Old Town, Waterfront, Industrial Zone, Luxury Hills and Airport District.
- Story idea: the player enters a new city, builds reputation through racing and missions, and investigates the cause of a major blackout.
- Supporting character roles and all character names.
- Exact mission structure, police behaviour, property ownership and business systems.
- Exact online robbery/theft rules, cooldowns, protection windows and transfer limits.
- Final logo composition, colour palette and slogan.
- Final monetisation providers and reward values.

## 7. Scope reality

The full concept is far beyond a first prototype. The first build should prove only the core technical loop: load a 3D scene in a browser, move a controllable vehicle, follow it with a camera, collide with simple obstacles and run on a phone at a stable frame rate. Everything else comes after that works.
